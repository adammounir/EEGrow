1 · The regime, not the leaderboard

12 MOABB motor-imagery datasets · 3 protocols · 14 pipelines · 5 seeds · 2100 runs · 189,062 scores

The single most reproducible thing in the grid is not which network wins but when a network wins at all. Within one subject's session there are a few hundred trials, and a Riemannian classifier — whose inductive bias already encodes the spatial covariance structure of the signal — beats a network that has to learn it. Only when training pools dozens of subjects does the order reverse.

What the chance-level audit changed

A paired delta says nothing unless both of its arms are decoding. We now test each arm against its own exact chance threshold — binomial for accuracy, Mann-Whitney for AUC, at that cell's actual number of units. 27 of the 120 growing-vs-fixed comparisons have at least one arm that does not clear it, and those 27 include the largest positive deltas in the whole grid.

Two claims in the v5 write-up no longer hold as written.

(a) EEGNeX is not a width-matched pair. In EEGNeX, filter_1 sizes the fixed tail as well as the block the growing arm widens, so the grown arm also ships a permanently narrower tail that growth never reaches. That pair contrasts two different target architectures; its −0.032 measures the missing tail. The honest count is three pairs, not four, until it is re-run matched.

(b) The whole grid ran a training protocol we have since shown to be wrong. Every cell used patience 20 with selection_monitor: valid_acc and nothing restored the best model — every score is the weights exactly 20 epochs past that arm's own best (std 0.0 across all 140,490 folds). On the budget × selection square that costs 0.339 against 0.482 at full budget, and 6 of the 9 arms audited are undertrained by +0.02 to +0.13. It does not flip the regime story, since it hits both deep families the same way. It does mean every new campaign passes train.patience=200 train.selection_monitor=valid_acc explicitly.

2 · Stage 1 — does our Euclidean Alignment reproduce the published effect?

Target fixed before the run: Junqueira, Aristimunha, Chevallier & de Camargo, arXiv 2401.10746, Table 2 — BNCI2014-001 LOSO, 68.93 → 73.98, Δ = +5.05 pp

The absolute level is deliberately not the test. Their harness is not ours — different early stopping, different selection rule, different seeds — so landing four points either side of 68.93 would say nothing. The paired per-subject delta is the test, because everything that separates the two harnesses is shared between our raw and our aligned arm and cancels in the difference. The unit of analysis is the held-out subject: sessions and seeds are replication layers and are averaged inside a subject before anything is computed.

{{fig:ea_gate}}

That is not a negative result — it is an underpowered one, and the difference matters. The minimum detectable effect at n = 9 is +6.69 pp, larger than the +5.05 pp the gate exists to check. A design that cannot see the effect it was built to test yields neither a pass nor a fail, and reporting the wide CI as "no effect" is exactly how an underpowered study gets written up as a null.

{{fig:ea_power}}

The answer, at 52 subjects

Same code, same estimator, four times the subjects: 3 networks × {raw, aligned}, one seed, a full 52-fold LOSO each, 44.6 GPU-hours, all six cells rc=0. One seed rather than three because at n = 52 the MDE is already 1.97 pp, and seeds do not buy power here.

The effect exists, and it is a third of the published one. The upper end of our CI, +2.83 pp, sits below +5.05 pp, so this is not a wide interval that happens to contain the published value — at this n we can say the effect on Cho2017 with our harness is smaller than the effect reported on BNCI2014-001. Part of that is headroom: our raw baseline is 73.33 % against the paper's 68.93 %. We are not claiming that explains all of it, and the subject-level version of that explanation does not hold up (Figure 8), so the gap is stated rather than explained away.

What the gate was for is nonetheless answered: our EA implementation does reproduce the published effect in direction and significance, which is what stage 2 needs before an interaction with growth can mean anything.

Best-effort means best-effort. gpu-best has PriorityTier 1, PreemptMode REQUEUE and a zero-second grace time, so any higher-tier job evicts ours instantly — and a requeued cell restarts at fold 0, because the resume guard keys on the final CSV and there is no per-fold checkpoint. On a 10-hour cell preempted every 90 minutes that is not a delay, it is a cell that never finishes. The EEGNet pair was caught by exactly that and moved to tau (tier 100, which our group has), which is why it lands six hours behind the rest. Every future campaign goes to tau directly.

These networks are latency-bound, not FLOP-bound. EEGNet is the most expensive of the three despite having the fewest operations, and a 2018 Turing card loses only a factor 1.60 to an H100 on the identical cell (81.7 s vs 131 s per fold, measured on a 10-subject probe). Kernel-launch overhead sets the pace. The practical consequence is that queueing for the three scarce H100s is almost always the wrong trade against starting now on six free Turing cards.
